Plastic Attention and Sensorimotor Habits

Online plasticity and the emergence of persistent sensorimotor organization.

Research question

Could an attention-based agent form and sustain its own sensorimotor habits through ongoing interaction, and if so, how would this differ from recurrent or explicitly plastic sensorimotor architectures?

Overview

Enactive accounts of cognition treat habits as patterns sustained and transformed by embodied activity, rather than as static rules stored inside an agent. This project asks whether attention mechanisms, when equipped with online, activity-dependent plasticity, can reproduce some of those dynamics. The distinction is important: retrieving relevant past experiences is not by itself evidence of a self-sustaining habit. The proposed model must change its own organization as a consequence of its enacted sensorimotor history.

Planned comparison

  • An iterant deformable sensorimotor medium (IDSM)-inspired baseline.
  • A plastic continuous-time recurrent neural network (CTRNN) or comparable recurrent baseline.
  • Attention with fixed representations or memory.
  • Attention with activity-dependent online plasticity.

Evaluation

Proposed tests include the formation and persistence of recurrent sensorimotor patterns, behavioral diversity, adaptation after perturbations, and whether habits remain stable without ongoing external reinforcement. The precise model class and operational definition of a habit are still being developed.

Status: conceptual research and design. These are proposed experiments, not completed results.
