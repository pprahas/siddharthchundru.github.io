Understanding Habits: The Enactive Perspective

Undergraduate research essay

Abstract

Habits have long been a focal point in psychological research, and have come to be characterized as automatic behaviors formed through repetitive stimulus-response associations and activated by environmental cues. This view, heavily influenced by dual processing theory and associationist principles, operates from within a mindless-mindful dichotomy that oversimplifies the complexity of actual habitual actions. Recent empirical evidence, which indicates that habits exhibit flexibility, adaptability, and can involve conscious deliberation, has come to challenge this perspective. This paper critiques the traditional cognitivist understanding of habits and explores the enactivist approach as a more comprehensive alternative framework. Enactivism, rooted in the organicist tradition, views habits as dynamic, self-organizing patterns emerging from an organism's embodied interactions with its environment. By positioning habits at the core of cognitive processes, enactivism emphasizes their fundamental role in shaping intelligent behavior. The paper finally reviews computational models employed by enactivists, demonstrating how these models provide valuable insights into the formation and maintenance of habits.
