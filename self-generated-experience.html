Learning from Self-Generated Experience

Experience generation, embodied learning, and generalization.

Research question

When agents must learn from limited interaction with their environments, does the policy that determines which experiences they collect matter as much as the learning algorithm itself?

Overview

This project studies how different experience-generation strategies affect learned sensorimotor representations and downstream behavior. Its starting point is a controlled two-dimensional navigation environment, where training conditions can be compared under matched data or interaction budgets. The broader motivation is to understand whether agents learn more transferable structure by acting purposefully, exploring randomly, or following intrinsic signals such as novelty and competence progress.

Planned comparison

  • Demonstrations or other fixed, externally collected experience.
  • Random exploration.
  • Goal-directed, outcome-sensitive experience generation.
  • Intrinsic exploration driven by novelty or learning progress.

Evaluation

The proposed evaluation examines model prediction error, state-space visitation or coverage, sample efficiency, and transfer to held-out goals or perturbed conditions. Experimental details, metrics, and baselines remain subject to revision as the implementation and literature review progress.

Status: research design and implementation in progress. No experimental results are reported here yet.
